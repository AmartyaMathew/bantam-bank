Operational resilience · evidence before estimates

Bantam cyber risk in pounds

This website acts an interactive demo to combine the MITRE ATTACK framework(helps answer how can an attack happen), with the FAIR framework(helps answer what is the chance of an attack and how much will it cost it is successful?). It assume a financial profile for Bantam, and provides three risk scenarios. Each scenario has estimated costs in case of a successful attack and costs to prevent said attack.

The idea is to attach financials to cybersecurity to help make more informed decisions and to help present cybersecurity beyond a cost center.

The Monte Carlo risk lab appears below the attack-tree explorer. It shows a range of possible losses for the scenarios mentioned.

Six layers, with a visible evidence boundary

  3. 03

    Human readable graphs of the components behind Bantam's backend logic.

  4. 04

    Attack-tree costing

    State the incident path over extracted Bantam flows. RTO is the time allowed to restore the service, so it drives the outage cost. RPO is the amount of data or state that may need to be rebuilt, so it drives the reconstruction cost. Add the response, remediation, and customer/regulatory costs to price the full path.

  5. 05

    FAIR-informed assumptions

    Represent loss-event frequency and magnitude explicitly. The demonstration’s probabilities are hypotheses for review, not outputs of the graph.

  6. 06

    Monte Carlo decision range

    Sample detection, dwell, and blast-radius uncertainty; compare the resulting annual loss distribution with the board’s tolerance and proposed control costs.

What the simulation is actually doing
  1. One year, played out ten thousand times. Nobody knows whether this attack will happen next year. So the page pretends the year happens ten thousand times over, and each pretend year rolls a couple of dice to decide what took place.
  2. The three sliders set the odds on those dice. How likely is a full traversal in a given year? How much does one cost when it lands? How much can the bank absorb before it counts as a breach? Move a slider and the simulation reruns with new odds.
  3. The results describe those ten thousand years. The expected annual loss is the average bill across all of them, most of which cost nothing. The worst year in a hundred is the top 1% — anything above that is rarer than a one-in-a-hundred year. The exceedance figure is how often the bill went over what the bank said it could absorb.
  4. This is arithmetic, not a prediction. The numbers are what the assumptions imply, not a forecast of what the bank will lose. Change an assumption and the arithmetic changes with it, which is the whole point.

    5 · Monte Carlo risk lab

    Change the assumptions, then rerun the year

    Same simulation as the mini lab above, with every input exposed. Each scenario row has its own prevention switch, so a reader can test payment, identity, or availability controls beside the assumptions they change. Nothing leaves the page: no values, graph data, or API keys are sent, and the lab never calls a model. A fixed seed makes the same inputs reproducible.

    Annual trigger

    The chance that the whole attack tree is successfully traversed at least once in a year. It is a business/threat assumption, not a count of bugs.

    Loss if triggered

    The chance that a successful traversal becomes a material incident after detection, containment, recovery, and customer impact are considered.

    Attack-tree cost

    The estimated bad-day bill: RTO outage cost plus RPO reconstruction cost plus response/remediation and customer/regulatory work.

    7 · Phased implementation roadmap

    Move from demonstrator to decision-grade evidence

    Months 1–2

    Define and calibrate

    Nominate important business services, owners and impact tolerances. Replace all three scenario probabilities and loss values with ranges supported by incident, threat and finance evidence.

    Months 2–4

    Close evidence gaps

    Generate signed CycloneDX/SPDX artifacts, ingest vulnerability age and exploitability, map Docker/host dependencies, and link Mistral attack-tree citations to the same immutable graph digest.

    Months 4–8

    Prevent and detect

    Harden privileged identity, dependency builds and release provenance. Centralize authentication, ledger, outbox and infrastructure signals with measurable detection and decision times.

    Months 8–12

    Prove recovery

    Exercise database restore, NATS failure, worker stalls, certificate rotation, compromised image rollback and Droplet loss. Record whether each service remained within tolerance.

    8 · Technical appendix

    How the simulation and rebuild work

    Analytic baseline

    EAL = P(scenario trigger) × P(loss | trigger) × attack-tree traversal cost

    The traversal cost is derived from explicit RTO/RPO and remediation assumptions. The three defaults produce £168k, £218.4k and £142.5k respectively: £528.9k before sampled severity factors.

    Sampled severity

    Detection is truncated normal, dwell is Weibull and blast radius is Beta. Each factor is normalized around its expected value and bounded, preventing a numerical tail from silently dominating the portfolio.

    Automatic graph rebuild

    HTML cannot parse a private repository by itself. A GitHub Actions workflow runs on every main push, verifies Bantam’s deterministic catalogue, builds a reduced public projection, and deploys the static artifact to Pages.

    Publication boundary

    The Pages artifact exposes graph identifiers, symbols, conditions and table-level effects. It excludes raw SQL, documentation bodies, credentials and runtime records, and contains no API key of any kind.
